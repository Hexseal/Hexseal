What it is

A client and a stranger can transact without trusting each other, or us

A client posts a job or hires against a posted service. Funds go into a per-deal escrow contract in USDC. Work is delivered, the client approves — or the timeout approves for them, so an absent client cannot strand an executor. Either side can raise a dispute; an arbiter takes it from an on-chain registry and rules. Completion pays out, awards reputation and mints a permanent record, all in the same transaction.

Users need no ETH: every action can travel as a signed meta-transaction that our relayer pays gas for. If the relayer is down the app falls back to the user's own wallet, so the relayer is a convenience, never a gate.

Working today on testnet

What is already built

Per-deal escrow

Each agreement is its own contract, cloned via EIP-1167 to keep deployment cheap. Funding, delivery, approval, timeout approval, refund.

Two-sided marketplace

Clients post jobs; executors post services. Both boards are on-chain, with regional pricing and supersede/cancel handled by the contracts.

Arbitration with skin in the game

Anyone can apply as arbiter behind a clean-streak gate and a forfeitable USDC bond. Claims and verdicts are commit-reveal, so an arbiter cannot shop for the case they want.

Appeals and recovery

A verdict can be appealed to a vote of other arbiters. A silent arbiter times out and the case returns to the pool instead of freezing the money.

Paid arbiter call

On small pots a percentage fee will not pay for fifteen minutes of reading, so a dispute goes untaken. The party who needs a judge tops the reward up to a working floor.

Earned reputation

XP is awarded by the contract on completion — not claimed, not granted. Clean streaks unlock the arbiter seat; a verdict against you costs you.

Protocol treasury

A separate immutable contract. Fees fill an arbiter-reward buffer first, then split by fixed constants. Reserve withdrawal is gated on earned unique active users, not on a flag we can flip.

On-chain records

A soulbound receipt is minted when a job is posted; the agreement itself is a permanent NFT with metadata and SVG rendered on chain, not hosted by us.

End-to-end encrypted chat

Our own transport, built to replace a third-party messenger. Keys derive from a wallet signature; messages are sealed envelopes in a hash-chain signed by a separate key; our server stores opaque bags it cannot read, and both parties keep proofs.

Trust model

What the contracts decide, and what a human still can

The interesting question about an "autonomous" protocol is not what it automates but what it still lets someone override. Here is the honest split.

DecisionWho holds itNotes
Release of escrowed funds Contract

Client approval, timeout approval, or an arbiter verdict. No key can move a deal's funds outside those paths.
Dispute verdict Contract

Commit-reveal claim and verdict, appeal vote, timeout recovery. We cannot pick the arbiter for a case.
Reputation Contract

Awarded on completion inside the same call. Not mintable, not adjustable.
Fee split and treasury ladder Contract

Percentages are compile-time constants; changing them means replacing the contract.
Which code runs (upgrade authority) Owner key

The known centralization point. The Diamond owner can replace any facet. Our own review found the treasury's reserve gate holds against a governance flag but not against this key — the cheapest bypass measured at ~31,700 gas and is invisible to standard Diamond introspection.
Who the first arbiters are Owner key

Deliberate launch decision: the first arbiters are seated by hand, under the same rules, bonds and penalties as everyone else. Self-application and governance stay in the code but are not switched on at launch.
Relayer, file storage, notifications Our servers

Liveness only, never custody. Chat storage is blind by construction; the app falls back to direct wallet transactions when the relayer is unavailable.

Stated plainly

Until the upgrade key is timelocked or burned, "autonomous" describes the protocol's logic, not its governance. We would rather an auditor read that here than discover it in the diff.

How it is built

The defects we expect are the ones we automated against

Two bug classes have cost this project real time, so both are now gates that fail the build rather than review items someone might notice.

GateCatches
Storage layout, three checks

Any change to a Diamond-storage field that is not a pure append — including a type swap inside the same slot, which is what actually broke our job board once. Covers namespace slots, facet variables, struct fields, and the cloned agreement layout.
Gasless sender, by AST

msg.sender reached for in a meta-transaction path, where it is the forwarder rather than the person. Parsed from the compiler's syntax tree, not by regex; every contract must be classified or the gate goes red.

Beyond that: 3,100+ automated tests across contracts, frontend and relayer; Slither on the contract set; and a review rule that a fix is only accepted with a measurement showing behaviour changed — because "fix this" gets satisfied formally, while "prove by measurement that it differs" does not. Findings that survive that bar are tracked in the open, including the ones still open.

Road to mainnet

What is left, in order

  1. Dispute evidencePresenting the encrypted conversation to an arbiter: original frames with per-message keys sealed to the arbiter's on-chain key, an anchor from each side, and divergence between anchors treated as a finding of its own. In progress.
  2. Hardening passClose the tracked open items, then freeze the contract set. Auditing a moving target wastes the audit.
  3. External auditFull review of the frozen set: escrow, arbitration and appeals, treasury, Diamond upgrade surface.
  4. Governance of the upgrade keyTimelock or hand off the authority named in the trust model above, so the mainnet claim matches the code.
  5. Mainnet betaReal USDC, capped deal sizes, arbiters seated by hand while the registry proves itself.
